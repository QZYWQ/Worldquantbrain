It sounds like you're on the right track by trying to diversify your alphas and targeting high-representation regions. However, to see an increase in your weight factor, you might want to focus on improving the quality and robustness of your alphas. Here are a few additional suggestions:

Evaluate Alpha Performance Across Different Market Conditions: Test your alphas in various market regimes (bull, bear, and sideways markets) to ensure they’re adaptable and not just optimized for specific conditions. This can help improve their overall performance consistency.

Refine Signal-to-Noise Ratio: Ensure your alphas are focused on high-value signals and not just capturing market noise. Try reducing unnecessary features or noise-prone data points and ensure that your alphas are statistically significant.

Incorporate Risk-Adjusted Metrics: Rather than focusing solely on returns, improve the risk-adjusted performance of your alphas (e.g., maximizing Sharpe ratio). Use risk management techniques such as volatility control or dynamic position sizing to boost the reliability and stability of your signals.

Explore Less Competitive Markets: In addition to the commonly represented regions like USA or EUR, consider exploring emerging markets or asset classes with lower competition. These areas may offer hidden opportunities that have been overlooked by more generalized strategies.

Focus on Long-Term Alpha Stability: Ensure your alphas are not just overfitting to short-term trends or recent market movements. A long-term, stable alpha will likely improve your weight factor over time as it demonstrates reliable, consistent performance.

By continuously evaluating and refining these aspects, you'll likely see improvements in your weight factor and overall performance.

Improve Alpha Performance: Consistent, strong performance over time is key to increasing your weight factor. This includes having a high Sharpe ratio, consistent returns, and minimal drawdowns. If your alphas are delivering strong results, they are likely to get higher weight in the platform’s evaluation.

Increase Alpha Contributions: Regularly contributing new alphas that perform well or show improvement in key metrics like fitness, out-of-sample performance, and robustness will help improve your weight factor. Additionally, improving the stability and diversification of your alphas over time is also important.

Reduce Turnover: Lowering your alpha’s turnover (number of trades) while maintaining its performance can help increase the weight factor. This reflects the stability and sustainability of the alpha, which is highly valued in the system.

Diversity and Coverage: Having a diverse set of alphas across multiple market segments (e.g., different sectors, regions, or factors) can boost the weight factor, as it reduces risk concentration and showcases your ability to apply multiple strategies.

Engage in the Community: In some systems, community involvement, such as commenting, discussing strategies, and sharing knowledge, can help increase your visibility and overall ranking, indirectly impacting your weight factor.

Report for Improvement:

While there currently isn’t an automated quarterly report detailing performance specifics, you can manually track your alpha performance over time and analyze which factors or signals have been performing the best. Keep an eye on your in-sample and out-of-sample results to identify weaknesses and areas for improvement. It may also be helpful to create a performance dashboard using external tools or even APIs to track your alphas over time.

The best way to improve for the next quarter is to continuously iterate on your models, tweak parameters, and experiment with new factors based on past performance.
